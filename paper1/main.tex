\begin{abstract}
Interpretability methods are usually trusted on the strength of checks that need no ground
truth: an estimate is believed if independent restarts agree on it, if two methods agree,
or if it fits held-out data. None of these checks has itself been calibrated against a
known answer, because the known answer is exactly what is missing. We point out one place
where it is not missing. The pre-activation of an MLP neuron is exactly the inner product of
its layer's normalised residual stream with the neuron's input weight column, so the
direction the neuron reads is written in the weights. Using this as a reference standard, we
score a single-index estimator related to maximally informative dimensions, and the checks
used to vouch for it, on 300 units of GPT-2 small, with replications across depth and model
family. The estimator misses the reference direction on 15\% of units. Agreement across
restarts predicts these failures worse than held-out fit (AUC 0.79 vs 0.92,
$p = 1.8\times10^{-4}$; pooled across six conditions, $\Delta$AUC $= -0.134$, 95\% CI
$[-0.189,-0.078]$). The advantage comes from under-fitted estimates. On the third of failures
that fit as well as the reference but point elsewhere, neither check is reliable. An
exploratory analysis traces these fits to a known identifiability limit: they differ from the
reference almost entirely in directions the stimulus barely occupies, and on held-out text
from the same distribution they predict the unit as well as the reference does.
\draft{Two signals that need no ground truth do flag them: the share of the fitted direction
that lies in the stimulus's lowest-variance directions (AUC 0.94 to 0.97 in five arms), and
agreement between fits on disjoint token samples (AUC 0.91 on 6 fits). Removing that share
repairs 40 of 43 such fits.} Restart
agreement does not improve with more restarts or when measured in the stimulus metric, and
single-unit verdicts are fragile: one in five flips when one source of randomness changes.
\draft{With response noise added so that the attainable fit varies between units, held-out
fit keeps its lead ($\Delta$AUC $-0.219$, 95\% CI $[-0.348,-0.110]$).} We
release the bench, the pre-registrations and every run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

When an interpretability method returns a direction, a feature or a circuit, the
practitioner rarely knows the right answer. The result is therefore trusted on the strength
of internal checks. A common one is agreement: run the method again from a different
random initialisation, or with a different seed for the dictionary, and keep what comes
back. Sparse-autoencoder features are judged by whether they reappear across seeds
\citep{song2025position}, and often they do not \citep{paulo2025sparse}; concept activation vectors by whether their scores
are stable across training runs \citep{kim2018tcav}, ICA and NMF components by whether
repeated runs cluster together \citep{himberg2004icasso,brunet2004metagenes}, and neurons
by whether they recur across independently trained models \citep{gurnee2024universal}. A
recent position paper asks the community to report run-to-run consistency routinely, as a
proxy for recovering ground truth \citep{song2025position}.

Agreement is a measure of how repeatable a result is. Whether it also measures whether the
result is right is an empirical question, and one that is hard to ask, because answering it
needs the ground truth whose absence motivated the check. Measurement science separates the
two properties explicitly. In the international vocabulary of metrology, \emph{precision}
is the closeness of agreement between repeated measurements, while \emph{trueness} is the
closeness of their average to a reference value, and trueness ``is not related to random
measurement error'' \citep{vim2012}. An instrument can be precise and wrong. Metrology's
answer is calibration: an instrument is checked against a reference standard before its
readings on unknowns are trusted, a principle that also governs high-assurance sensing,
where sensor readings are trusted only through a verifiable chain back to a reference
\citep{shah2020calibration} and devices are authenticated by their physical signature
\citep{vaidya2022acoustic}. The pattern is familiar from benchmarking too: metrics that
suffice for a simple setting become ``necessary, but also not sufficient'' once the system
under test is more complex \citep{dholakia2025agentic}. Agreement across restarts was
designed for a simpler question, whether an optimiser converged, and it is now read as
an answer to a harder one, whether the converged answer is right.

This paper supplies a reference standard for one interpretability instrument and uses it to
calibrate the checks practitioners rely on. The standard is free. An MLP neuron's
pre-activation is $z = \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{s} + b$, where $\mathbf{s}$ is the output of
the layer norm that feeds the MLP and $\mathbf{w}$ the neuron's input weight column. With
respect to $\mathbf{s}$, the neuron reads exactly one direction, and that direction can be
read off the weights (Section~\ref{sec:substrate}). Any method that claims to recover what a
unit responds to from stimulus and response alone can be scored against it, on units shaped
by training rather than built by hand, in unlimited supply.

We use it to ask a narrow question with a broad reach: \emph{when a direction-recovery
estimator fails, which of the checks available without ground truth notices?} Our
contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A calibration bench.} Exact per-unit ground truth on real trained language
  models, an estimator from the receptive-field literature
  \citep{sharpee2004mid,williamson2015equivalence}, four ground-truth-free reliability
  checks, and a pre-registered protocol for scoring the checks by how well they predict
  failure (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}).
  \item \textbf{Restart agreement is the weaker check.} Held-out fit predicts failure better
  in each of the six conditions with at least five failures, with a pooled gap of 0.13 AUC.
  The lead shrinks but holds when restart agreement is scored on the estimation route it
  actually measures (Section~\ref{sec:results}; every run is listed in
  Appendix~\ref{app:arms}).
  \item \textbf{The hard case is an identifiability limit.} Neither check detects fits that
  converge to a direction other than the reference. In an exploratory analysis these fits
  differ from the reference in directions the stimulus barely occupies, and they are
  equivalent to it on held-out text from the same distribution
  (Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}). \draft{They can still be flagged without ground truth,
  by the fitted direction's share in the lowest-variance stimulus directions and by agreement
  across token samples rather than across restarts (exploratory; a filed test on new units is
  pending \tbd{X-1}).}
  \item \textbf{More restarts are not a remedy.} Restart agreement does not improve as
  restarts are added, and about a quarter of units are not recovered by the direct route
  within four random restarts (Section~\ref{sec:restarts}).
  \item \textbf{An error budget.} Verdicts move with restart count, token sample, held-out
  split and hardware; failure rates move with depth and model family. We report each
  component, so that a single quoted failure rate carries its conditions.
\end{enumerate}
We do not claim that models resemble brains. We claim that interpretability and systems
neuroscience share a measurement problem, and that the discipline neuroscience built for it,
calibration against a preparation where the answer is known, transfers.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Stability as a reliability check.} Agreement across random restarts or resamples
is a long-standing proxy for reliability in unsupervised estimation: run-to-run clustering
of ICA components \citep{himberg2004icasso}, consensus clustering for NMF
\citep{brunet2004metagenes}, and, in interpretability, seed consistency of sparse-autoencoder
features, which is often low \citep{paulo2025sparse,gerasimov2026unstable} and has been
proposed as a routine report \citep{song2025position}. Song et al.
validate run-to-run consistency against ground truth on synthetic dictionaries, where it
tracks ground-truth recovery over training in aggregate \citep{song2025position}; we ask
whether agreement identifies \emph{which individual} estimates are wrong, on real trained
models. Gerasimov et al. find that seed-stable SAE features carry most of the reconstruction
signal while unstable ones reflect reproducible subspaces resolved differently per seed
\citep{gerasimov2026unstable}; our results are compatible, since stability can track
importance without tracking correctness. Warnings that stability is not correctness exist
in clustering \citep{bendavid2006sober}, and the failure of agreement between independently
built versions as evidence of correctness is classic in software reliability
\citep{knight1986nversion}. Appendix~\ref{app:census} lists 34 published uses of agreement as a
reliability check across eight sub-areas.

\paragraph{Predictability before stability.} The PCS framework for veridical data science
uses ``predictability as a reality check'', screens models on prediction before assessing
stability, and lists random initial values in gradient descent among the perturbations
stability should survive \citep{yu2020veridical}. Our result is a ground-truth test of that
ordering: the predictability check outperforms the stability check. Restart agreement
estimates how much of the initialisation distribution flows into a basin, which is evidence
about the basin's size rather than its correctness \citep{nehzati2026restarts}.

\paragraph{Ground truth in interpretability evaluation.} Benchmarks with known answers
plant or compile them \citep{gupta2024interpbench}, or synthesise features
\citep{song2025position,chanin2026synthsaebench}. Reading an MLP's input columns as the
patterns its neurons detect goes back at least to \citet{geva2021keyvalue}; we use them as a
scoring key for methods that never look at weights.

\paragraph{Evaluating the evaluations.} Closest to our aim are audits of the metrics
themselves. \citet{chanin2026benchmarks} audits the metrics of SAEBench
\citep{karvonen2025saebench} against reseed noise and synthetic ground truth, and
SynthSAEBench plants realistic features at scale and reproduces the gap between
reconstruction and latent quality \citep{chanin2026synthsaebench}.
\citet{makelov2024principled} scores sparse autoencoders against supervised dictionaries.
Probing has control tasks \citep{hewitt2019control}, and automated interpretability scores
have been found not to separate trained from random networks \citep{heap2025random}. These
works plant or supervise their ground truth and mostly score methods. Ours is read off a
trained network's weights, and we score the checks.

\paragraph{Identifiability in receptive-field estimation.} Systems neuroscience has long known
that a filter estimated from natural stimuli is determined only within the subspace the
stimulus explores. Normalised reverse correlation divides by the stimulus covariance and has
to regularise its poorly sampled directions \citep{theunissen2001strf,wu2006sysid};
spike-triggered estimators are consistent only under conditions on the stimulus distribution
\citep{paninski2003convergence}; and priors are used to fill the unexplored directions
\citep{park2011localized}. Methods there are also validated on systems whose answer is known
\citep{jonas2017microprocessor}. Section~\ref{sec:identifiability} finds the same limit in
trained language models, where it makes a Euclidean reference standard imperfect.

\paragraph{Misleading metrics elsewhere.} Widely adopted checks have misled before:
saliency maps that pass visual inspection yet are insensitive to the model
\citep{adebayo2018sanity}, adversarial defences that look robust until evaluated adaptively
\citep{athalye2018obfuscated}, and security classifiers whose standard metrics hide failure
under evasion \citep{gardiner2016security,arp2022dos}. Construct reliability is not construct
validity \citep{jacobs2021measurement}, and evaluating AI systems is a measurement problem
\citep{wallach2025position}.

\section{An exact reference standard for unit directions}
\label{sec:substrate}

Let $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the residual stream entering an MLP block,
$\mathbf{s} = \mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{x}) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}\odot\mathbf{u} +
\boldsymbol{\beta}$ the output of its layer norm, where $\mathbf{u}$ is the normalised,
zero-mean input, and $W_{\text{in}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\text{mlp}}}$ the MLP's input
weights. Neuron $j$'s pre-activation is $z_j = \mathbf{w}_j^\top\mathbf{s} + b_j$ and its
response is $y_j = \mathrm{GELU}(z_j)$. As a function of $\mathbf{s}$, the response depends
on exactly one direction, $\mathbf{w}_j$. We verify the identity numerically
(Table~\ref{tab:verify}).

\paragraph{One direction is not identifiable, and we remove it.} Because $\mathbf{u}$ has
zero mean across coordinates, $\mathbf{s}\cdot(\boldsymbol{1}/\boldsymbol{\gamma})$ is
constant on every token. The component of $\mathbf{w}_j$ along $\boldsymbol{1}/\boldsymbol{\gamma}$
only shifts the bias and cannot be recovered from data. In GPT-2 small its median share of
$\|\mathbf{w}_j\|^2$ is about $5\times10^{-4}$; at layer 6 no unit has a share large enough
to cap the attainable cosine below our bar, at layer 2 four of 3{,}072 do. We report alignment
against $\mathbf{w}_j$ and, where fitted directions were saved, against its identifiable
part. The GPT-2 layer-6 run did not save fitted directions; every later run did.

\paragraph{Scope.} The standard holds for a unit reading its own layer's layer-norm output.
It does not extend to residual-stream features, sparse-autoencoder latents or behavioural
directions, and whether calibrations transfer to them is not established here. A
pre-registered attempt on sparse-autoencoder latents of GPT-2 failed at the estimator, which
recovered 3 of 100 latents, so the checks could not be compared there
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{The instrument and the checks}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stimulus and response.} We stream public-domain prose (Project Gutenberg) through
the model in 128-token sequences, drop position 0, and record $\mathbf{s}$ and $y_j$ for
8{,}000 tokens at the chosen layer. Units are drawn uniformly from the layer's 3{,}072.

\paragraph{Estimator.} A rank-$k$ bottleneck regression $y \approx g(V^\top \mathbf{s})$,
with $V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}$ orthonormalised by QR and $g$ a two-hidden-layer tanh
network of width 64, fitted by Adam (learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$, 1{,}600 steps) on
standardised inputs and responses, with 20\% of tokens held out and early stopping on
held-out $R^2$. This is a single-index model fitted by least squares. It is related to
maximally informative dimensions \citep{sharpee2004mid} through the equivalence between
information-theoretic and likelihood-based estimators \citep{williamson2015equivalence}. The first restart is warm-started from a
ridge-regression direction; the rest are random. We use two routes to a rank-1 answer:
\emph{direct} (fit $k{=}1$) and \emph{cascade} (fit $k{=}2$, then search the best direction
within the plane), and select between them by held-out $R^2$. Units are fitted in batches
with per-unit initialisation and per-unit early stopping, so a unit's fit does not depend on
its batch-mates (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

\paragraph{Failure.} A fit fails when $|\cos(\hat{\mathbf{v}},\mathbf{w}_j)| < 0.95$. We also
report threshold sweeps from 0.90 to 0.98 and alignment as a continuous outcome.

\paragraph{Checks scored.} Each is computable without $\mathbf{w}_j$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{held-out $R^2$} of the selected fit. It is a validation score: the same held-out
  tokens drive early stopping and the choice between routes;
  \item \emph{restart agreement}, the median pairwise $|\cos|$ between restarts of the direct
  route. Most runs use two restarts, the first warm-started from a ridge direction, so this
  is a single cosine. The cascade route has no restarts, and it supplied the selected fit for
  140 of the 300 primary units (Section~\ref{sec:matched});
  \item \emph{restart $R^2$ spread}, max minus min held-out $R^2$ across restarts;
  \item \emph{route agreement}, $|\cos|$ between the direct and cascade directions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Protocol.} The primary endpoint, filed before the run, is DeLong's test for two
correlated ROC curves \citep{delong1988comparing}: AUC of restart agreement minus AUC of
held-out $R^2$ at predicting failure, on the same units. Secondary analyses (paired bootstrap,
PR-AUC, threshold sweep, calibration, nuisance baselines with incremental cross-validated AUC,
permuted-label and ground-truth-signal controls) were filed before the run's outputs were
opened, and the analysis code was developed on an earlier, already-unblinded run.
Appendix~\ref{app:status} classifies every reported result as confirmatory, pre-specified
secondary or exploratory.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The estimator fails on a minority of units}
Of 300 GPT-2 layer-6 units, 254 pass (0.847, 95\% CI $[0.80, 0.88]$) and 46 fail, while the
median alignment across all units is 0.994 and the minimum 0.047; aggregate statistics hide
the failures. Thirty failures are under-fitted (held-out $R^2 \le 0.99$). Sixteen reach held-out
$R^2 > 0.99$ but point away from $\mathbf{w}_j$; we call these converged-wrong, the name
the pre-registration uses.
Classical closed-form estimators fail almost completely on the same units: spike-triggered
averaging recovers 0 of 30, decorrelated STA 1 of 30 and spike-triggered covariance 0 of 30
(as a symmetric generalised eigenproblem), against 26 of 30 for the fitted estimator, because the stimulus is non-Gaussian and GELU is
non-monotone over the range these units occupy. A random-direction null bounds chance fit at
$R^2 \le 0.044$.

\subsection{Restart agreement is a weaker predictor of failure than held-out fit}
Restart agreement predicts failure with AUC 0.792 and held-out $R^2$ with 0.919; the
difference is $-0.127$ (DeLong $p = 1.8\times10^{-4}$; paired bootstrap 95\% CI
$[-0.193,-0.063]$; a paired permutation test that swaps the two checks within units gives
$p = 0.0002$). The ordering holds at every failure
threshold from 0.90 to 0.98, and in precision--recall terms the gap is larger (PR-AUC 0.77
against 0.32 at a failure prevalence of 0.15); see Figures~\ref{fig:roc} and
\ref{fig:classes} (left).

\paragraph{Matching the checks' targets.}\label{sec:matched} Restart agreement looks only at the
direct route, while the failure label scores the selected fit, which came from the cascade
route for 37 of the 46 failures. On the 160 units where the direct route was selected, the
gap is $-0.113$ (9 failures). Scored instead against the direct route's own alignment, the
estimate restart agreement actually measures, the gap halves to $-0.061$ (107 failures). Part
of the headline gap is therefore a mismatch between the checks' targets. Held-out $R^2$ still
leads under every matching we tried, in every arm with enough failures
(Appendix~\ref{app:arms}).

\subsection{Restart agreement adds little beyond unit difficulty}
Four difficulty descriptors of each unit (fraction of tokens on which it is active, mean
pre-activation, response kurtosis, and the GELU first-order coefficient) predict failure on
their own with cross-validated AUC 0.825. Adding held-out $R^2$ raises this to 0.892; adding
restart agreement leaves it at 0.831. Three of those descriptors are computed from the true
pre-activation, so they are oracle covariates that a practitioner cannot compute. With
descriptors computed from the response alone (active fraction, mean, standard deviation and
kurtosis), the baseline scores 0.806 on GPT-2. Held-out $R^2$ adds 0.074 to it (bootstrap 95\%
CI $[0.027, 0.125]$) and restart agreement adds 0.011 ($[-0.013, 0.043]$). On Pythia-160m the
increments are 0.051 ($[-0.027, 0.167]$) and 0.003 ($[-0.024, 0.048]$), and on GPT-Neo layer 10
neither check adds anything (exploratory; 50 repeats of 5-fold cross-validation, with units
grouped across folds in the bootstrap). On GPT-Neo-125m neither check adds anything
over the oracle descriptors (0.707 alone, 0.699 with held-out $R^2$, 0.703 with restart
agreement), because half its failures are converged-wrong fits, which neither check detects
(Section~\ref{sec:classes}).

\subsection{The gap is consistent across models, layers and families}
Table~\ref{tab:arms} pools six conditions that share no units. Absolute AUCs vary widely,
from a held-out-$R^2$ AUC of 0.76 on GPT-Neo to 0.97 on Pythia-1.4B, but the gap varies
less. Random-effects pooling gives $\Delta$AUC $-0.134$, with a Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman
95\% CI of $[-0.189,-0.078]$ and a 95\% prediction interval of $[-0.194,-0.074]$. Pooling only
the four corrected-estimator arms gives $-0.134$ $[-0.212,-0.057]$. Six arms give little power
to detect heterogeneity ($Q = 1.05$), so we read $I^2 = 0$ as an absence of evidence. Held-out
$R^2$ is ahead in all six point estimates (Figure~\ref{fig:forest}), but the stratified
bootstrap interval includes zero for Pythia-160m and GPT-2 layer 10 (Table~\ref{tab:arms}).
Two further arms, Pythia-70m and Pythia-410m, had two failures each and are not pooled; on
Pythia-70m restart agreement is ahead ($+0.25$, interval $[-0.10, +0.67]$;
Appendix~\ref{app:arms}). Four arms
were fitted with the corrected estimator; the two GPT-2 depth arms are earlier runs whose fits
were coupled across units (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Re-running the GPT-2 layer-6,
Pythia-160m and GPT-Neo arms on the corrected estimator moved their gaps from $-0.164$,
$-0.094$ and $-0.133$ to $-0.127$, $-0.123$ and $-0.167$.

\subsection{The checks catch under-fitting; neither catches converged-wrong fits}
\label{sec:classes}
Split by failure class (pre-registered before the run's outputs were viewed), held-out $R^2$'s
advantage is entirely the under-fitted class: AUC 0.993 $[0.985, 0.999]$ against 0.796
$[0.729, 0.858]$ for restart agreement. That part is close to mechanical, because the class is
defined by held-out $R^2 \le 0.99$ and only 18 of the 254 passing units fall below that line.
On the sixteen converged-wrong fits the two checks are tied: 0.779 $[0.700, 0.849]$ and 0.782
$[0.664, 0.872]$. The split is unchanged for class boundaries between 0.98 and 0.995. On
GPT-Neo-125m, where converged-wrong fits are 20 of 38 failures, both checks are at chance on
them (held-out $R^2$ 0.55 $[0.42, 0.68]$, restart agreement 0.47 $[0.33, 0.61]$) and both work
on the under-fitted class (0.98 and 0.72). At 1.4B every failure was under-fitted. In the words
of the filed reading rule, neither check sees the hard case. Its size is 5\% of units on GPT-2
small and 20\% on GPT-Neo layer 10.
\draft{The two classes have different causes. Under-fitted units are sparse: they fire on a
median 2 to 4\% of tokens against 12 to 21\% for passing units, and their response standard
deviation is a half to two-thirds as large, so the fit has few informative tokens. In four of five arms
converged-wrong units fire about as often as passing units (7 to 17\%) and have the same
response spread; what separates them is geometry, not data quantity (exploratory).}

\subsection{Why converged-wrong fits escape: identifiability (exploratory)}
\label{sec:identifiability}
The reference standard of Section~\ref{sec:substrate} is exact in weight space, but a
stimulus-response estimator can only recover a direction within the subspace its stimulus
explores, a limit familiar from receptive-field estimation \citep{theunissen2001strf,
paninski2003convergence,wu2006sysid}. The residual stream is strongly anisotropic
\citep{timkey2021rogue}. Across our arms the stimulus covariance $C$ has a condition number
between $1.8\times10^{4}$ and $1.3\times10^{9}$, and the bottom 1\% of its variance is spread
over 90 to 102 of the 768 directions. The analyses below test whether this explains the
converged-wrong class. They were filed as exploratory after the class split had been seen.

For runs that saved their fitted directions we compared each fitted direction
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ with $\mathbf{w}_j$ by their cosine under $C$,
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}^\top C\mathbf{w}_j / (\hat{\mathbf{v}}^\top C\hat{\mathbf{v}}\,
\mathbf{w}_j^\top C\mathbf{w}_j)^{1/2}$, which equals the correlation of their projections over
tokens. We call it stimulus-weighted alignment, and measured it on the fitted tokens and again
on 8{,}000 tokens from documents the fit never saw. On the fresh text, converged-wrong fits
reach a stimulus-weighted alignment of at least 0.99 in every arm we examined: 20 of 20 on
GPT-Neo layer 10; 5 of 6, 6 of 7 and 7 of 8 in three re-fits of the primary GPT-2 units; and 1
of 2 on Pythia-160m (Table~\ref{tab:functional}). Their leave-one-out fit there falls short of
$\mathbf{w}_j$'s by a median of 0.0004 to 0.0035, while their Euclidean alignment is only 0.91
to 0.94. Decomposing each error $\hat{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{w}_j$ across the eigen-directions of
$C$ shows where the difference lies. In the three arms we decomposed, 81 to 96\% of a
converged-wrong fit's error energy sits in the directions carrying the bottom 1\% of stimulus
variance. A random vector would put 12 to 13\% there, and under-fitted fits put 20 to 55\%.
A GPT-Neo layer-6 run is the extreme case. All 20 units fail the Euclidean bar, at a median
alignment of 0.01, yet their stimulus-weighted alignment is 0.9998 and restarts agree at 1.0.
\draft{Its cause differs from the other arms. The reference columns there are ordinary: their
median share in the bottom-1\% variance directions is 0.15. Instead, one output coordinate of
the layer norm is nearly constant, with standard deviation $1.65\times10^{-5}$ against a median
of 0.083. The estimator standardises each coordinate before fitting and maps the result back by
dividing by that coordinate's standard deviation, so whatever weight the fit places on the
constant coordinate, which the data cannot constrain, is magnified about 5{,}000-fold and
carries almost all of the fitted direction's norm. Each fitted direction is then a small
multiple of $\mathbf{w}_j$ plus a large component that never changes the projection, which is
why the projections agree while the Euclidean cosine is near 0.01. Restart agreement is 0.99 or
higher on 19 of the 20 failures, so here agreement is confidently wrong. Across layers,
GPT-Neo-125m has such coordinates at layers 0 to 9 and GPT-2 only at layer 3; every other layer
fitted in this paper has none. This is an artifact of the estimator's scaling, not of the
units. \tbd{B-17b/c/d: re-fit with the coordinate dropped at GPT-Neo layer 6, and the predicted
case at GPT-2 layer 3.}}

\paragraph{\draft{A ground-truth-free diagnostic.}} \draft{The analysis above suggests a check a
practitioner can compute: the share of the fitted direction's squared norm that lies in the
eigen-directions carrying the bottom 1\% of stimulus variance. It separates converged-wrong
fits from passing ones with AUC 0.953 on GPT-Neo layer 10 (20 fits), 0.941, 0.961 and 0.947 on
the three GPT-2 re-fits (6, 7 and 8 fits) and 0.972 on Pythia-160m (2 fits), where held-out
$R^2$ scores 0.55 to 0.72 and restart agreement 0.47 to 0.85. The same share computed for
$\mathbf{w}_j$ does not separate them (AUC 0.17 to 0.50), so it describes the fit, not the
unit. Projecting each fitted direction onto the remaining 99\% of variance repairs 40 of the
43 converged-wrong fits (all 20 GPT-Neo layer-6 fits as well). Over all failures the share is
weaker than held-out $R^2$ (0.73 to 0.88 against 0.76 to 0.95), so it complements held-out fit
rather than replacing it. A second signal agrees. For the 100 re-fitted GPT-2 units, the
absolute cosine between a unit's directions fitted on two different token samples predicts
failure with AUC 0.922 (0.911 on the 6 converged-wrong fits), against 0.726 for restart
agreement and 0.896 for held-out $R^2$. Converged-wrong fits differ from $\mathbf{w}_j$ in
directions the data barely determine, so those components move when the tokens change but not
when only the initialisation does. Both results were found after the data were seen, rest on
few converged-wrong fits, and the two token-sample fits also differ in restart count (five
against two). A filed test on new units, each fitted twice on document-disjoint samples with
identical settings, is pending \tbd{X-1}.}

Two limits apply. First, the equivalence is predictive and in-distribution. Steering or
patching along $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ pushes activations into exactly the low-variance directions
where $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ and $\mathbf{w}_j$ differ, and interventions that act through such
dormant directions are known to mislead \citep{makelov2024subspace}. Second, the
stimulus-weighted label is not independent of held-out $R^2$, since both depend on how
$\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ projects the stimulus. Table~\ref{tab:functional} therefore reports AUCs
under a functional failure label only as a sensitivity analysis: held-out $R^2$ scores 0.97 to
0.98 under it largely by construction. What the analysis does establish is narrower. On this
substrate the Euclidean reference standard is imperfect, and its imperfection concentrates in
the very class that both checks miss.

\subsection{More restarts do not fix it}
\label{sec:restarts}
Restart agreement computed from the first $k$ of five stored restarts does not predict
failure better as $k$ grows, and stays below held-out $R^2$ at every $k$
(Figure~\ref{fig:restart}). This holds in two coupled-estimator runs (6 and 7 failures) and
in a corrected-estimator re-fit of 100 primary units (18 failures), where restart agreement
scores 0.74, 0.70, 0.69 and 0.73 at $k = 2$ to 5 against 0.90 for held-out $R^2$. Other
operationalisations do no better. In the same re-fit, agreement over the four random restarts
alone scores 0.70, and agreement measured as the cosine under the stimulus covariance scores
0.66, against 0.73 as filed (exploratory).

Pass rates are inconsistent with a single per-restart success probability. In the coupled
runs, a single probability predicts 97.5--98\% at five restarts against an observed 88--91\%.
In the corrected re-fit, the direct route recovers 50, 66, 70 and 72\% of units within one to
four random restarts. A model with a share of units that no restart reaches fits these rates
far better than a single probability (AIC 511.6 against 528.3). So does a model in which each
unit has its own success probability (AIC 512.0), and the data cannot tell the two apart. We
therefore claim only that about a quarter of units are not recovered by the direct route
within four random restarts. The cascade route recovers some of them, so 18\% of selected
verdicts fail, and in an earlier step-budget sweep each of six hard units reached an alignment
of at least 0.93 at some budget. Recovery is limited by this estimator and its budget, not
ruled out.

\subsection{An error budget for single-unit verdicts}
\label{sec:budget}
We re-fitted the primary run's first 100 units three times on the corrected estimator,
changing one thing each time: the fit seed together with the restart count (five instead of
two), the 8{,}000-token sample, or the held-out split, which became sequence-level. Against
the original verdicts, these flip 19\% (95\% CI 12--28\%), 25\% (17--35\%) and 23\% (15--32\%)
of them, and verdict agreement is only slight to fair (Cohen's $\kappa$ 0.34, 0.17 and 0.27),
although the failure count barely moves (17 originally, then 18, 20 and 22). At a 0.90 bar the
flip rates fall to 6--14\%, so many flips are units near the bar. Per-unit alignment, compared
as an angle, reproduces moderately across seeds (ICC 0.68) and poorly across token samples
(0.33) or splits (0.24). The run-level comparison is steadier: held-out $R^2$ leads restart
agreement in all three re-fits, by 0.169, 0.084 and 0.162 (DeLong $p = 0.011$, 0.14 and
0.017), against 0.123 for the original fit of the same units. Earlier runs add two
components: changing the restart count from two to five changes 13 to 16\% of verdicts, and
running on a GPU instead of a CPU changes 4 of 50. Pass rates differ with depth (0.50, 0.86,
0.90 at layers 2, 6, 10) and with model family at fixed depth (GPT-Neo-125m 0.62 vs GPT-2
0.90 at layer 10). A failure rate should therefore be quoted with its configuration.
\draft{Across the four fits of these 100 units, 56 never fail, 22 fail once, 15 twice, 3 three
times and 4 every time. Units that always fail have a median alignment of 0.76; units that
fail only sometimes sit near the bar, at 0.967. Of the 18 units that are converged-wrong in at
least one fit, only 2 fail in all four, so most failures are near-threshold instability rather
than a fixed property of the unit (exploratory).}

\subsection{\draft{Held-out fit keeps its lead under response noise}}
\label{sec:noise}
\draft{Our main responses are noiseless, so the reference direction attains $R^2 = 1$ on every
unit and a low held-out $R^2$ is unambiguous. In a pre-registered experiment we added Gaussian
noise to the responses of the primary run's first 100 units, re-fitted them, and repeated the
primary comparison. In the primary arm each unit drew its own signal-to-noise ratio between 1
and 19, so the attainable $R^2$ ranged from 0.50 to 0.95 and was unknown to the checks. 74 units
failed. Held-out $R^2$ scored 0.971 and restart agreement 0.752, a gap of $-0.219$ (bootstrap
95\% CI $[-0.348,-0.110]$, DeLong $p = 0.0004$, paired permutation $p = 0.0002$), larger than the
$-0.084$ to $-0.169$ of the noiseless re-fits. Noise lowers held-out $R^2$ and causes failures
together, so a low value still marks the units that fail; dividing held-out $R^2$ by each unit's
ceiling, which a practitioner cannot do, lowers its AUC to 0.829. At a fixed signal-to-noise
ratio the gap keeps its sign but its interval includes zero: $-0.087$ $[-0.200, 0.024]$ at 19
(34 failures) and $-0.078$ $[-0.419, 0.181]$ at 4, where 95 of 100 units fail and the
comparison has almost no power. Noise mostly adds under-fitted failures; converged-wrong fits
number 1 at a ratio of 19, 8 in the primary arm and 14 at a ratio of 4.}

\section{Why agreement can be confidently wrong}
\label{sec:why}

Restart agreement measures how much of the initialisation distribution flows into the basin
the estimator ends in. If a wrong basin is large, restarts agree on it, and agreement grows
with the number of restarts exponentially faster than evidence that the basin is the only one
\citep{nehzati2026restarts}. Single-neuron regression with a biased, non-monotone activation is
a known source of such basins: gradient descent on a single neuron with bias fails from a
large fraction of initialisations \citep{vardi2021learning}, and the units that fail here sit
in GELU's non-monotone region, where the first-order signal that guides early optimisation
nearly vanishes. Held-out fit, by contrast, measures the basin's loss. Because our
responses are noiseless functions of $\mathbf{w}_j^\top\mathbf{s}$, the true direction attains
$R^2 = 1$ on every unit we inspected, so a wrong basin pays for its wrongness in fit and the
predictability check sees what the stability check cannot. The fits that do not pay are the
converged-wrong ones, which differ from the reference only where the stimulus has almost no
variance (Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}). \draft{With noisy responses the attainable fit
varies between units and a wrong basin no longer pays in an unambiguous way, yet held-out fit
kept its lead (Section~\ref{sec:noise}), because the noise that lowers the fit is also what
makes those units fail. Agreement can also be confidently wrong outright: in the GPT-Neo
layer-6 run, restarts agree at 0.99 or above on 19 of 20 failures. Elsewhere its weakness is
the opposite, false alarms: on the primary run no failure reaches agreement 0.99, but 186 of
254 passing units fall below it.} Label-free
estimates of correctness are never guaranteed \citep{garg2022leveraging}; held-out fit is a
necessary condition, not a certificate.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
We expected the hard case to be a fit that is wrong yet fits perfectly, and it exists:
neither check detects it. On this substrate the exploratory analysis says why. These fits
differ from the reference only where the stimulus has almost no variance, so the data cannot
separate them from it. Receptive-field estimation from natural stimuli meets the same limit
and handles it with priors over the unexplored directions \citep{park2011localized}. For
interpretability the consequence is practical. A direction recovered from activations is
pinned down only within the subspace those activations explore, and an agreement check
cannot reach outside it, however many restarts it uses. Detecting the failures that remain has
the same shape as detecting unreliable behaviour where our understanding of the offending
behaviour, in per-instance labels, is sparse, as in click-fraud detection from traffic
analysis \citep{nagaraja2019clicktok}. Security research has long treated
reliability signals adversarially, assuming a check will be evaded until shown otherwise
\citep{gardiner2016security,arp2022dos}; interpretability's reliability checks have not yet
been held to that standard.

Transfer is the open question. Our pre-registered attempt on GPT-2 sparse-autoencoder latents,
whose encoder rows give an exact reference in the same way, did not reach the comparison. At
the largest budget we could afford (16{,}000 tokens, 3{,}200 steps), the estimator recovered 3
of 100 latents, and 93 of the 97 failures were under-fitted. Restart agreement and held-out
fit then scored 0.945 and 0.979 against only 3 passing units, which says nothing about either
check. The check practitioners actually rely on for sparse autoencoders, agreement across
training seeds, needs a substrate with planted features to be calibrated
\citep{chanin2026synthsaebench}.

\section{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The reference standard covers MLP units reading their own layer. A pre-registered
  transfer attempt to sparse-autoencoder latents failed at the estimator (3 of 100 recovered),
  so the calibration is untested beyond MLP units.
  \item Models are small: 124M to 160M parameters, plus a single 1.4B arm that needed twice
  the optimisation budget.
  \item One estimator family, a single-index model fitted by least squares. The checks are
  estimator-agnostic, but their AUCs are not.
  \item Responses are noiseless functions of the reference direction, so the attainable fit is
  known and equal to 1 for every unit. \draft{A pre-registered noise experiment on 100 primary
  units keeps the lead when that ceiling varies by unit; at a single fixed noise level the gap
  keeps its sign but its interval includes zero (Section~\ref{sec:noise}). The noise is
  Gaussian and added to the response, not produced by the model.}
  \item \draft{The estimator standardises each stimulus coordinate. Where a coordinate is
  nearly constant this creates failures of its own (Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}); every
  pooled arm is free of such coordinates.}
  \item The 0.95 bar is Euclidean, and the stimulus leaves part of each reference direction
  unidentifiable (Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}). Threshold sweeps and a functional label
  are reported as sensitivity analyses.
  \item In most runs restart agreement uses two restarts of one route, the first warm-started
  (Section~\ref{sec:matched}), and held-out $R^2$ is a validation score reused for early
  stopping and route selection.
  \item Most runs hold out tokens from the same sequences as their training tokens. A
  sequence-level split of 100 primary units leaves held-out $R^2$ unchanged (median shift
  $2\times10^{-6}$) and its lead over restart agreement intact.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We calibrated the checks that interpretability uses in place of ground truth against a
reference standard that trained models supply for free: the input weight column of an MLP
neuron. Restart agreement is the weaker of the two checks we compared most closely. It trails
held-out fit in every pooled condition and does not improve with more restarts or in a
better-suited metric. Held-out fit's advantage comes from under-fitted estimates. Estimates
that fit well but point away from the reference escape both checks, and on this substrate
they differ from the reference only where the stimulus has almost no variance. The practical
advice is short: report held-out fit for every estimate, do not read agreement across
restarts as evidence of correctness, and treat a recovered direction as determined only
within the subspace its stimulus explores. \draft{Where a check beyond held-out fit is wanted,
perturb the data rather than the initialisation, and measure how much of the recovered
direction lies where the stimulus has almost no variance.} A reliability check is itself a measuring
instrument, and like any instrument it should be calibrated against a known answer before its
readings are trusted.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{app:threats}
\paragraph{Construct.} Failure means a Euclidean cosine below 0.95 to $\mathbf{w}_j$. Three
things could make that label mislead.
(i) The layer-norm null direction cannot be recovered. At layer 6 its ceiling is at least
0.951 for every unit, so no verdict there is decided by it.
(ii) The residual stream is anisotropic. A direction can differ from $\mathbf{w}_j$ only in
directions the stimulus barely occupies and still predict the response as well. This happens:
it is what the converged-wrong fits are (Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}). For the runs with
saved directions we computed four labels per unit: the raw cosine, the cosine to the
identifiable part of $\mathbf{w}_j$, the stimulus-weighted alignment, and the same alignment
restricted to tokens where the unit fires. The first two differ by a median of 0.001 and at
most 0.027, so (i) decides nothing. The stimulus-weighted label moves failures both ways. It
clears 24 of 38 Euclidean failures on GPT-Neo, 5 to 8 of 18 to 22 on the GPT-2 re-fits and 1
of 13 on Pythia. It also fails 0 to 4 units per run that pass the Euclidean bar. The
firing-region alignment of the converged-wrong fits has a median of at least 0.997, so the
agreement does not come from tokens where the unit is silent.
(iii) The recorded response is the exact (erf) GELU of the hooked pre-activation, while
GPT-2 uses a tanh approximation. This changes the link slightly and the reference direction
not at all.
The failure-class split is defined by held-out $R^2$, and the functional label depends on
the same projections, so neither is independent of the held-out $R^2$ check. Both are
reported beside the pre-registered Euclidean label, not in its place.
\paragraph{Internal.} Fit quality and failure share a cause, unit difficulty, so held-out
$R^2$ could predict failure only by restating difficulty. We report nuisance-only baselines
and the incremental AUC of each check over them; three of the four original descriptors use
the true pre-activation, so we also report a baseline built from the response alone. The coupling defects in
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} are fixed in the primary run. Held-out tokens share sequences
with training tokens in most runs; repeating 100 primary units under a sequence-level split
changes held-out $R^2$ by a median of $2\times10^{-6}$ and leaves its lead intact.
\paragraph{External.} The models are small and come from three families, two of which
(GPT-Neo and Pythia) share a training corpus. The units are
GELU MLP neurons reading a layer-norm output, the stimulus is English prose, and there is one
estimator family. The checks themselves are estimator-agnostic, but their AUCs need not be.
The one transfer attempt, to sparse-autoencoder latents, failed at the estimator.
\paragraph{Statistical conclusion.} DeLong's variance estimate is unstable when there are few
failures. Every primary comparison is therefore also given as a paired bootstrap and a
label-permutation test, and arms with fewer than five failures are not pooled. Pooled
estimates combine arms that share no units, under a random-effects model. Verdicts on single
units move with restart count, token sample and hardware. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports
those components, so that a failure rate carries its conditions.

\section{The bench}
\label{app:bench}
\benchname{} ships what is needed to score any direction-recovery method, or any reliability
check, against the reference standard of Section~\ref{sec:substrate}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the stimulus recipe: corpus, document and token seeds, 128-token sequences with
  position 0 dropped, and the 8{,}000-token budget;
  \item the unit lists for every model and layer used here, with each unit's reference column
  and its layer-norm null direction;
  \item every fitted row, the saved fitted directions and restarts, and the stimulus
  diagnostics of Section~\ref{sec:identifiability};
  \item the estimator and the command line that regenerates each arm, the analysis scripts,
  every pre-registration and the laboratory notebook.
\end{itemize}
A new check is scored by computing it for each unit's fit and passing the per-unit values to
the analysis script, which returns its AUC against the reference label and its paired
comparison with held-out $R^2$. \tbd{licence and anonymised link}.

\section{Usage census}
\label{app:census}
We searched for published uses of agreement across restarts, seeds or resamples as grounds
for trusting a result, choosing a model or its size, or reporting robustness. We verified 34
instances across eight sub-areas:
\begin{itemize}
  \item sixteen use agreement to validate or trust a result;
  \item twelve use it to select a hyperparameter, a model or the number of components;
  \item nine report it as robustness.
\end{itemize}
Some instances do more than one of these. A few, such as convergent learning and universal
neurons, ask whether independently trained networks converge, which is a scientific question

\section{Classical baselines and the chance level}
\label{app:baselines}
On 30 GPT-2 layer-6 units, closed-form receptive-field estimators recover almost nothing
(Table~\ref{tab:classical}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the spike-triggered average is unbiased only for spherically symmetric stimuli, and
  decorrelating it helps only partly;
  \item spike-triggered covariance needs a symmetric nonlinearity.
\end{itemize}
These units sit in GELU's non-monotone region over a non-Gaussian stimulus, which defeats all
three.

For the chance level, we drew 2{,}000 random unit directions on 12 units (20{,}000 tokens).
Their median alignment with $\mathbf{w}_j$ is 0.024 (99th percentile 0.091; the isotropic
expectation in 768 dimensions is 0.036). Through a binned link, their $R^2$ reaches at most
0.044 at the 99th percentile, against a median of 0.92 for the true direction under the same
link. A fit with high held-out $R^2$ is therefore never a chance direction.
