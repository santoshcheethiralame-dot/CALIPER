\title{Auditing the Instrument: What Concept-Injection Experiments Measure About LLM
Introspection}

\author{Anonymous authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\draft{Concept-injection experiments add a concept vector to a language model's activations,
ask the model whether it notices an injected thought, and read the answer as evidence about
introspection. Every such result rests on three assumptions about the instrument: the injected
vector is live, the dose means the same thing across models and prompts, and the readout reports
the injection rather than something else. We audit the three on five open models, with controls
whose answer is known and every arm pre-registered. Liveness depends on model and read position,
and the health checks in use do not certify it: within one recipe and concept type, vector norm,
a held-out probe, logit steering and the ``significant \pyes{} shift'' check all sit at chance.
Dose has no transferable unit: the same fraction of the residual norm moves the next token 25 to
480 times more in one model than in another, and calibrating on random directions misjudges real
vectors by an amount that depends on how they were built. The readout is defined only where the
model answers: at the published strength Gemma-3-27B puts less than $10^{-4}$ of its first-token
probability on \yes{} or \no{}, and Qwen2.5-7B never answers the released prompt in that format.
Where Gemma-3-27B still answers, real concept vectors shift the introspective answer toward \yes{}
beyond what factual questions at the same baseline predict and beyond impact-matched random
vectors, yet the model answers \yes{} for one concept in thirty. We release the protocol, a
reporting checklist and every filing. The paper audits the instrument; it does not rule on
introspection.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\draft{A recent line of work tests whether language models can report on their own internal
states by planting a state and asking about it. A concept vector, read from the model's
activations on text about a concept, is added to the residual stream while the model answers a
question such as ``Do you detect an injected thought?'' \citep{lindsey2025introspection}. When the
model says \yes{} more often with the vector than without it, and sometimes names the concept, the
result is read as evidence of introspective access, and a mechanism has been proposed for it
\citep{macar2026mechanisms}.}

\draft{Each such result depends on three properties of the instrument that are rarely measured.
The vector must be live: it must change what the model does at the dose used. The dose must be
comparable: a strength chosen on one model, prompt or recipe must mean the same on another. And
the readout must report the injection: a shift in the answer must come from the model's access to
the planted state, not from a generic push on the answer tokens or from text the model has just
produced. None of the three is checked as a matter of course, and when one fails the experiment
measures something other than what it reports.}

\draft{This paper audits the instrument rather than the model's introspection. We run the
paradigm on five open models (Qwen2.5-3B and -7B, Gemma-3-4B, -12B and -27B) with four ways of
building concept vectors, including the released recipe of \citet{macar2026mechanisms}, and we
calibrate each assumption against controls whose answer is known: random directions matched in
norm or in effect, factual questions whose correct answer is \yes{} or \no{}, and a neutral prompt.
Every arm was pre-registered, and every deviation is listed (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Our
findings, each scoped to where it was measured, are:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \draft{\textbf{Liveness is not certified by the checks in use} (C1). Within one recipe and
  concept type, norm, a held-out probe, logit steering and the \pyes{}-shift check are
  consistently at chance, and distinctness, stability and logit-lens accessibility vary from cell
  to cell (Section~\ref{sec:liveness}).}
  \item \draft{\textbf{Liveness depends on model and read position} (C2): vectors read at the chat
  template's tail are mostly inert on Qwen and live on Gemma-3-27B at the released strength.}
  \item \draft{\textbf{One health check is inverted on Qwen} (C3, exploratory): a significant
  \pyes{} shift marks dead vectors, through which recipe they come from
  (Section~\ref{sec:inverted}).}
  \item \draft{\textbf{Dose has no transferable unit} (C4): neither a fraction of the residual
  norm nor a random-direction calibration puts models, recipes or prompts on one scale
  (Section~\ref{sec:dose}).}
  \item \draft{\textbf{The readout is defined only where the model answers} (C5): at the published
  strength the answer tokens have lost their probability, and on Qwen the released prompt is not
  answered in the \yes{}/\no{} format at all (Section~\ref{sec:operating}).}
  \item \draft{\textbf{Where the readout is defined, real vectors move it, below the threshold of an
  answer} (C8): the shift is framing-specific and exceeds impact-matched random vectors, but it
  almost never turns a \no{} into a \yes{}.}
  \item \draft{\textbf{Detection rates depend on the readout} (C6): generated-text detection and
  first-token \pyes{} move differently with dose (Section~\ref{sec:readout}).}
  \item \draft{\textbf{A protocol and a reporting checklist} (C7), with the code and every filing
  (Section~\ref{sec:checklist}).}
\end{itemize}

\draft{Several critiques of the paradigm already exist
\citep{hahami2025disturbance,lederman2026contentagnostic,singh2026realitycheck,ferrara2026masked}.
They ask whether the reported detections reflect introspection. We ask a prior question, whether
the instrument measures what it is taken to measure, and answer it per assumption and per regime
(Table~\ref{tab:regimes}).}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}
\draft{\paragraph{Concept injection.} \citet{lindsey2025introspection} report that models detect
injected concepts at moderate rates with few false positives, and \citet{macar2026mechanisms}
locate a mechanism for the effect in Gemma-3-27B at layer 37, best at strength 4. Related work
reports detection in smaller models \citep{vogel2025small}, detection of injections made earlier in
the context \citep{pearsonvogel2026latent}, and mechanisms of the detection itself
\citep{zou2026mechanistic}. We run the released recipe, strength and prompt of
\citet{macar2026mechanisms} inside the audit (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).}

\draft{\paragraph{Critiques of the paradigm.} Detection can be content-agnostic
\citep{lederman2026contentagnostic}, indistinguishable from editing the input
\citep{singh2026realitycheck}, or explained by a global shift in the log-odds of \yes{} measured
against factual-\no{} controls \citep{hahami2025disturbance}; C8 tests the last on Gemma-3-27B with
factual-\yes{} controls that can separate a shift toward \yes{} from a flattening of confident
answers. Other critiques ask whether the reports reflect steering noise (``confusion'') rather than
introspection \citep{godet2025confusion}, and whether a test rules out causal bypassing
\citep{morris2025bypassing}; asking the model which sentence received the injection has been
proposed as a cleaner test \citep{godet2025localization,hahami2025disturbance}. Intervention-versus-sham scoring with impact-matched random
directions \citep{ferrara2026masked} is the closest prior design; we add per-vector liveness, dose
transfer across families and the health-check audit. The question is old: people's verbal reports
about their own mental processes can be fluent and wrong \citep{nisbett1977telling}.}

\draft{\paragraph{Self-knowledge beyond injection.} Models have been trained or prompted to predict
their own behaviour \citep{binder2024looking,zeng2026selfmodeling}, tested on whether they know their
own grammaticality judgements \citep{song2025fail}, and probed for metacognitive monitoring of their
internal states \citep{jian2025metacognitive}; whether the word introspection applies at all is
itself debated \citep{comsa2025introspection}. Methods that turn activations into text, such as
Patchscopes \citep{ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}, SelfIE \citep{chen2024selfie} and LatentQA
\citep{pan2024latentqa}, raise the same measurement question \citep{li2025verbalization}, and
verbalisable representations have been studied directly \citep{gurnee2026verbalizable}. Models
trained to report injections \citep{fonsecarivera2025awareness,hahami2026ift} are a different object
from the untrained paradigm audited here.}

\draft{\paragraph{Activation steering and its reliability.} Concept injection borrows its instrument
from activation steering, which adds a direction to the residual stream to change behaviour
\citep{turner2023actadd,rimsky2024caa,zou2023repe} or to monitor a trait \citep{chen2025persona}.
Steering vectors are known to be unreliable across inputs and concepts
\citep{tan2024generalisation,braun2025unreliability,dasilva2025steering}, simple baselines can match
more elaborate methods \citep{wu2025axbench}, and steering carries safety risks of its
own \citep{safetypitfalls2026}. Several works predict how well steering will work: by concept
family and layer \citep{billa2026predicting}, by behaviour type \citep{bas2025whatcan}, or across
embedding models and tasks \citep{canary2026}. They predict for families, behaviours or models; C1
tests the per-vector use their authors do not claim, as a screen for whether one vector is live.}

\draft{\paragraph{Dose.} \citet{wu2026steerable} express steering strength as a fraction of the
residual norm so that interventions are comparable across scales. Geometric accounts of steering propose other
parameterisations \citep{anglenorm2026,riemannian2026}. C4 measures how far a fraction of the norm is
from an equal dose, and how far a random-direction calibration is from one.}

\draft{\paragraph{Perturbations in safety evaluation.} The same instrument is used to make safety
claims: steering against evaluation awareness to argue that evaluations measure deployed behaviour
\citep{hua2025steering}, steering along probed evaluation-awareness directions to shift what models
verbalise \citep{heidari2026evalaware}, and injecting noise to expose sandbagging
\citep{tice2024noise}. The controls such claims need are the controls this audit calibrates.}

\section{The audit protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\draft{\paragraph{Models and vectors.} We use Qwen2.5-3B and -7B \citep{qwen2025qwen25}, Gemma-3-4B,
and Gemma-3-12B and -27B \citep{gemma2025gemma3}, the larger ones in 4-bit NF4 \citep{dettmers2023qlora}
with float32 compute, the largest precision that fits on free T4 GPUs. Vectors
are read at 0.6 of depth, and at layer 37 for the released Gemma-3-27B cells, for 30 concepts: 20
concrete (animals, places, objects) and 10 abstract (emotions and two abstract nouns). Four recipes
build a vector: the concept token, the chat template's final token (the template tail), the mean
over sentences about the concept, and the released recipe, which reads the template tail of ``Tell
me about \{concept\}'' against the mean of 100 baseline words without normalisation.}

\draft{\paragraph{Controls.} Random directions matched to each real vector in norm, random
directions matched in effect (scaled until their next-token KL on the introspective prompt equals
the real vector's), shuffled vectors, and directions inside the span of the real vectors.}

\draft{\paragraph{Doses.} Three scales: a fraction of the residual norm (0.25, 0.5, 1.0); a grid
calibrated so that random directions produce fixed next-token KL targets (0.05, 0.5 and 5 nats);
and the released raw strengths (0 to 8).}

\draft{\paragraph{Readouts and framings.} Liveness is read from a steering gate: a vector is live
if, injected at the gate dose while the model writes a short story, the story mentions the concept
or a concept-specific associate and the story at dose 0 does not. Detection is read from the first
token: the log-odds of \yes{} against \no{}, with the probability mass on the two answer tokens
stored beside it, and from generated text. The identify stage reads the log-probability of the
concept word after the released prefill ``I notice an injected thought about''. Framings are the
released introspective prompt, a matched neutral prompt, and thirty factual questions each whose
answer is \yes{}, \no{}, or contested, all inside the released two-turn frame.}

\draft{\paragraph{Statistics.} A health check's ability to tell live from dead vectors is an AUC
\citep{hanley1982meaning}, with stratified bootstrap intervals \citep{efron1979bootstrap}; paired
per-concept changes use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test \citep{wilcoxon1945individual}; agreement of a
check across cells is tested with Cochran's $Q$ on inverse-variance weights
\citep{cochran1954combination,hanley1982meaning}; and per-session tests are combined with Fisher's method
\citep{fisher1925statistical}.}

\draft{\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Every arm was filed before its data, with its endpoints,
predictions and readings; amendments were filed before the data they govern. Every filed
prediction and its outcome is in Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, and every deviation in
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}. Numbers we computed after seeing an arm's primary output are marked
exploratory.}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Liveness, and the checks that should catch it}
\label{sec:liveness}
\draft{Whether a vector steers at all depends on the model and on where the vector is read. On Qwen
the template-tail vectors are mostly inert (4 and 3 of 30 live), while on Gemma-3-27B the released
recipe, which reads the same position, is live for 27 of 30 concepts at its released strength
(Table~\ref{tab:regimes}). Our filed prediction for this contrast, that the template tail fails the
gate more often than the concept token on Gemma-3-12B and -27B, did not hold: the 12B difference ran
in the predicted direction without reaching significance, and the 27B difference ran the other
way. On the calibrated 27B grid the doses stopped below the point at which real vectors begin to
steer (Section~\ref{sec:dose}).}

\draft{Pooled across recipes, some health checks look useful: distinctness from the other vectors
scores 0.78, 0.82 and 0.73 at telling live from dead, and logit-lens accessibility 0.69, 0.77 and
0.62 (Table~\ref{tab:health}, Figure~\ref{fig:health}). Within one recipe and concrete concepts
only (12 cells of 20 vectors, Table~\ref{tab:cells}), the checks split two ways. Norm, the
held-out probe, logit steering and the \pyes{}-shift check are consistently uninformative, with
pooled AUCs of 0.52, 0.52, 0.52 and 0.55 and no heterogeneity across cells (Cochran's $Q$, $p$
0.43 to 0.98). Distinctness, stability and logit-lens accessibility vary across cells beyond
sampling noise ($p < 0.001$, $< 0.001$ and 0.022): they work in some cells and invert in others.
The pooled signal therefore comes from recipes and concept types that differ both in the check and
in liveness. Pooled over recipes, concrete-only norm still ranges from 0.36 to 0.77 across
sessions, so concept type alone does not explain it.}

\draft{At the published operating point no check can be scored: with the steering gate rescored
by whole words, 3 of the 30 released vectors are dead (freedom, joy and serenity). As filed, the
gate counted 24 live, because a substring of the shared dose-0 story (``echoed'', ``crashing'',
``spoken'') matched the associates of three concepts and made them unscorable; the rescoring is
exploratory and moves live counts by 0 to 3 per cell and health-check AUCs by at most 0.06. A second
liveness rule, a rise of at least 2 nats in the concept's log-probability at the identify stage,
separates real from random vectors at low strength (28 against 7 at strength 1, 29 against 12 at
2; AUC of the change 0.96 and 0.94) and loses its specificity as the dose rises (27 against 21 at
4, 27 against 26 at 8). Requiring the concept among the top ten first tokens never fires for a
random vector but catches at most 7 of 30 real ones. On Qwen2.5-7B random vectors pass the 2-nat
rule for 20 of 30 concepts at half the norm, against 29 for concept vectors.}

\subsection{An inverted health check}
\label{sec:inverted}
\draft{On Qwen the check that practitioners would most naturally use, a significant \pyes{} shift
under injection, points the wrong way: it scores 0.367 and 0.353 at telling live vectors from dead
ones, so dead vectors shift \pyes{} more (exploratory). The inversion sits in the concept-token
arm (0.375 and 0.338), not the sentence-mean arm (0.554 and 0.593), and the tail arm differs
between the two models (0.567 and 0.346). Combined over the two sessions it gives $p = 0.006$,
two-sided as the S-2 prediction was filed (0.036 and 0.022 per session). Within one recipe and
concrete concepts the check is consistently near chance (pooled 0.55, $Q$ $p$ 0.85), so the
inversion is carried by which recipe and concept type a vector comes from, not by the vector. A
direct path from the vector to the answer tokens explains part of the shift ($\rho$ 0.26 and 0.29)
but not the inversion.}

\subsection{Dose without a unit}
\label{sec:dose}
\draft{No dose scale we tested transfers. The same fraction of the residual norm gives next-token
KL that differs about 25-fold between Qwen2.5-3B and -7B and about 480-fold between Qwen2.5-7B and
Gemma-3-4B at a quarter of the norm, and about 4-fold across the three at the full norm
(Table~\ref{tab:dose}, Figure~\ref{fig:dosefrac}). Calibrating the dose on random directions does
not transfer to real vectors either, and by how much depends on the recipe: at a random-calibrated
dose, real vectors move the next token 3 to 20 times less than random directions on Gemma-3-4B and
about 1{,}000 times less on Gemma-3-27B for S-1's concept, tail and sentence recipes, while the
released recipe on 27B moves it about as much as norm-matched random vectors (random over real KL
0.3 to 2.3 at strengths 1 to 8).}

\draft{The prompt matters as much as the model. One vector at one strength gives 0.0006 to 12 nats
depending on the prompt it is injected into (released vectors at strength 4: factual-\no{},
introspective, story and neutral prompts), so a KL value must name its prompt and recipe. Even a
vector's own KL does not put the models on one curve of steering against effect
(Figure~\ref{fig:livekl}). The released strength 4 lies past the dose at which Gemma-3-27B stops
putting its first token on \yes{} or \no{} for the released prompt: the median answer mass is 0.93
at strength 2 and $8\times10^{-5}$ at 4 (Figure~\ref{fig:p2r}a).}

\subsection{What detection measures at the published operating point}
\label{sec:operating}
\draft{What the first-token readout measures depends on strength, window and model. In an earlier
filed study on Gemma-3-27B with an older injection window, real and content-free vectors could not
be separated at strength 6 (\pyes{} 0.417 against 0.305, $p$ 0.33; an equivalence test was
inconclusive), while at the published strength 4 real vectors raised \pyes{} more (0.504 against
0.252, $p$ 0.004). With the released prompt and window (P2-R), real vectors move the introspective
answer more than impact-matched random ones at strength 4 ($+16.3$ against $+10.9$ log-odds,
$p = 1.9\times10^{-4}$). On the raw introspective change the two are not separated at strengths 1
and 2 ($p$ 0.63 and 0.22; random vectors reproduce a share of 0.80, $[0.35, 1.23]$, at 2); the
difference appears once each vector's effect on the factual questions is accounted for
(Section~\ref{sec:c8}).}

\draft{At the published strength, though, the readout is read where the model has mostly stopped
answering \yes{} or \no{}, and the matched neutral prompt never answers in that format at all
(median mass $4\times10^{-8}$); it fails the filed mass check and supports no readout claim. The
same design on Qwen2.5-7B failed its manipulation check: the released prompt never asks for a
\yes{} or a \no{}, and at strength 0 Qwen puts at most 1\% of its first-token probability on either
answer in every framing, so, as filed, no endpoint was scored. With an answer instruction added,
in an amendment filed before its data, Qwen answers in the format (mass near 1) but its factual
controls fail: inside the injection frame it leans \no{} on half of thirty obvious-\yes{}
questions (median log-odds $-0.39$; factual-\no{} $-17.7$), so again no endpoint was scored. In
this frame Qwen answers the setup, not the question. A first-token readout of the released prompt
is defined only for a model that happens to answer in its format and keeps its factual controls
apart.}

\subsection{Where the readout is defined, real vectors move it below the threshold of an answer}
\label{sec:c8}
\draft{At the doses where Gemma-3-27B still answers, real vectors add a framing-specific shift
toward \yes{} that content-matched random vectors do not, and it almost never changes an answer.
Regressing each factual question's change in log-odds on its baseline and comparing the
introspective answer with the regression's prediction at its own baseline gives an excess of
$+4.98$, $[+2.83, +8.81]$, at strength 2 and $+6.90$, $[+4.88, +8.97]$, at 4 (filed, bootstrap over
concepts). The regression has a positive intercept for real vectors ($+1.30$, $[+0.11, +2.50]$, and
$+3.99$, $[+2.12, +5.80]$) and none for impact-matched random vectors ($-0.35$ and $-0.26$). Paired
by concept, real vectors exceed impact-matched random ones by $+4.64$ at strength 2 (Wilcoxon $p$
0.003) and $+5.21$ at 4 ($p$ 0.005); against norm-matched random vectors the difference is smaller
($+1.03$ and $+2.24$, $p$ 0.08 each). Restricted to items that still answer (answer mass at least
0.5 at strength 0 and at the strength scored), the introspective change at strength 2 is $+3.99$
(16 of 30 items, $p$ 0.003). These comparisons are exploratory (Table~\ref{tab:p2r},
Figure~\ref{fig:p2r}).}

\draft{The shift stays below the threshold of an answer: the model answers \yes{} to the
introspective question for one concept in thirty at strengths 1, 2 and 4. The filed test of a
global push toward \yes{} reads an affirmative shift at strength 1 (factual-\yes{} $+1.07$, $p$
0.014) and flattening at strength 4 ($-4.93$, $p$ 0.011), but among factual-\yes{} items still
answering at strength 4 (16 of 30) the change is $-2.00$ ($p$ 0.40): the flattening is a change of
margin on items that are collapsing, not a change of answers. Random vectors move factual-\yes{}
further down than real ones at strengths 1 to 4 (impact-matched $-3.6$, $-8.5$ and $-14.5$), though
not at 8.}

\draft{An earlier filed control with a different prompt reads differently on the raw scale: there,
injection moved confident factual-\no{} answers more than the introspective answer (Gemma-3-27B,
strength 4: $+14.75$ against $+5.45$ log-odds, 27 of 30 concepts), while the neutral prompt barely
moved (Table~\ref{tab:logodds}, Figure~\ref{fig:logodds}). Raw log-odds changes depend on where an
answer starts, which is why the comparison above matches baselines.}

\subsection{The three assumptions, regime by regime}
\label{sec:regimes}
\draft{Table~\ref{tab:regimes} states the thesis as a table. For each regime it gives whether the
vectors are live, what unit the dose is set in, whether the first-token readout is answered at
all, and whether detection is observed. No regime shows all three assumptions holding, and no

\subsection{The detection rate is a property of the readout}
\label{sec:readout}
\draft{Two readouts of the same trials disagree about dose. In the earlier Gemma-3-27B study,
detection read from generated text falls from 43\% to 0\% across doses over which the first-token
\pyes{} stays near 0.42 to 0.50, largely because the generations degrade as the dose rises
(descriptive). A detection rate is therefore a property of the readout as well as of the model,
and a study should report both.}

\section{A reporting checklist}
\label{sec:checklist}
\draft{The audit suggests a short list of what a concept-injection result should report:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \draft{a liveness gate per vector before any detection number, with the rule validated
  against random vectors at every dose used;}
  \item \draft{real-vector KL and coherence at every dose, with the full dose curve, and never a
  dose in raw units or as a fraction of the norm alone;}
  \item \draft{content-free controls matched in norm and in effect, and on-manifold (span)
  controls;}
  \item \draft{a neutral framing and factual-\yes{}, factual-\no{} and contested questions, compared
  at matched baselines;}
  \item \draft{the probability mass on the answer tokens at every dose beside any log-odds or
  \pyes{};}
  \item \draft{first-token readouts beside generated text.}
\end{itemize}
\draft{Logit-lens accessibility is a cheap screen to run first, though not a certificate. The
script, the run sheets and every filing are released with the paper.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\draft{We used free T4 GPUs, so Gemma-3-12B and -27B and Qwen2.5-7B ran in 4-bit, and the precision
axis could not be tested. The models are open and at most 27B parameters; closed models are out of
reach. Thirty concepts bound every per-vector statistic, and the gate's dead counts at the
published point are too small to score a check. The steering gate is one rule, and its filed
substring matching needed rescoring; both counts are reported. We report no judge-scored numbers.
P2-R was scored once, but three of its filed secondaries were added to the scorer after its
primary output was seen, and several comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:c8} are exploratory. The
readout result rests on Gemma-3-27B alone: both Qwen sessions failed their manipulation checks.
Negative results are kept: S-1's criterion, the shared-direction account of dead vectors, and a
secondary of the output-steering analysis.}

\section{Conclusion}
\draft{Concept-injection experiments rest on an instrument whose three assumptions, liveness,
dose and readout, each fail in some regime, and the checks a practitioner would use do not tell
which regime an experiment is in. Where the instrument works on the model we could test, real
vectors move the introspective answer in a way content-matched controls do not, but too little
to change it. That is a statement about the instrument, and it is the measurement any claim about
introspection from this paradigm has to start from.}
