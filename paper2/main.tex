\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\note{The paradigm: inject a concept vector, ask the model whether it detects an injected
thought \citep{lindsey2025introspection,macar2026mechanisms}. Three assumptions every such result
rests on, none checked in the literature. What the paper does: an audit of the instrument, not
a verdict on introspection. Contributions C1--C8 from the outline, one line each.}

\note{Positioning in one sentence: critiques exist
\citep{hahami2025disturbance,lederman2026contentagnostic,singh2026realitycheck,ferrara2026masked};
this paper's contribution is the instrument (liveness, dose, health checks), not another
verdict.}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}
\note{Outline \S3 table, as prose. Detection results and the defended mechanism
\citep{lindsey2025introspection,macar2026mechanisms}. Content-agnostic and input-editing
critiques \citep{lederman2026contentagnostic,singh2026realitycheck}. The global affirmative
shift \citep{hahami2025disturbance} (C8 tests it). Cross-scale dose confounds and the
residual-norm convention \citep{wu2026steerable}, whose authors note it "does not guarantee equal
functional dose across models"; C4 measures how far it is from equal (25--480x in KL at 0.25 of the
norm). Steerability predictors \citep{billa2026predicting,braun2025unreliability,bas2025whatcan}
rank concept families and layers; C1 tests a per-vector use their authors do not claim (logit-lens
accessibility as a liveness screen for one vector). Intervention-vs-sham scoring with
impact-matched controls \citep{ferrara2026masked} is the closest prior design. Trained detection \citep{fonsecarivera2025awareness,hahami2026ift} is a
different object.}

\section{The audit protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\note{Models: Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Gemma-3-4B/12B/27B, Gemma-2-2B for A-F1. Recipes: concept token,
template tail, sentence mean, released. Controls: norm-matched random, impact-matched random,
shuffled, span. Doses: fraction of residual norm, KL-calibrated grid, released raw strengths.
Readouts: steering gate (rule-scored), first-token \pyes{}, rule-scored text. Live rule: steered
at the gate dose and not at dose 0. Every arm pre-registered; amendments and deviations in
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}. Hardware: free T4s, 4-bit for 12B and 27B.}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Liveness, and the checks that should catch it}
\label{sec:liveness}
\note{C2. Whether a vector steers at all depends on model and read position: template-tail
vectors are inert on Qwen, the KL-calibrated 27B grid stopped below the dose onset, and the
released recipe at its released strength is live on Gemma-3-27B (Table~\ref{tab:liveness}).
Present as the failed S-1 prediction, with its 12B reversal under a literal-word steering rule.}

\note{C1. Pooled across recipes some checks look useful: distinctness (0.78, 0.82, 0.73) and
logit-lens accessibility (0.69, 0.77, 0.62) (Table~\ref{tab:health}, Figure~\ref{fig:health}).
Within one recipe and concrete concepts only (12 cells of 20 vectors, Table~\ref{tab:cells}) the
checks split two ways. Norm, the probe, logit steering and the \pyes{}-shift check are
consistently uninformative: pooled AUC 0.52, 0.52, 0.52 and 0.55, with no heterogeneity across
cells (Cochran's $Q$ $p$ 0.43--0.98). Distinctness, stability and logit-lens accessibility vary
across cells beyond sampling noise ($Q$ $p < 0.001$, $< 0.001$, 0.022): they work in some cells
and invert in others. Pooled over recipes, concrete-only norm still ranges 0.36--0.77 across
sessions, so concept type alone does not explain it. At the published point, the steering gate
rescored with whole-word matching leaves 3 of 30 released vectors dead (freedom, joy, serenity),
too few to score any check; as filed it counted 24/30 live, because a substring of the shared
dose-0 story made three more concepts unscorable. The identify stage's 2-nat rule separates real
from random vectors at low strength (28 against 7 at strength 1, 29 against 12 at 2; AUC of the
change 0.96, 0.94) and loses specificity as the dose rises (27 against 21 at 4, 27 against 26 at
8); requiring the concept among the top ten first tokens never fires for random vectors but
catches at most 7/30 real ones. On Qwen2.5-7B random vectors pass the 2-nat rule 20/30 at 0.5 of
the norm, against 29/30 for concept vectors (P2-Q2 beside P2-Q). Steering-gate counts here use
the rescored gate (exploratory); it moves live counts by 0--3 per cell and health-check AUCs by at
most 0.06.}

\subsection{The inverted \pyes{} check}
\label{sec:inverted}
\note{C3 (exploratory). On Qwen a significant \pyes{} shift predicts a dead vector, not a live one
(AUC 0.367, 0.353). The inversion is in the concept-token arm (0.375, 0.338), not the
sentence-mean arm (0.554, 0.593); the tail arm differs between models (0.567, 0.346).
Fisher-combined over the two sessions $p$ 0.006 two-sided, as the S-2 prediction was filed
(0.0019 one-sided); per session 0.036 and 0.022. Within one recipe and concrete concepts the
check is consistently near chance (pooled 0.55, $Q$ $p$ 0.85), so the inversion is carried by
which recipe and concept type a vector comes from, not by the vector. A direct output-steering
path explains part of the shift (P2-M, $\rho$ 0.26, 0.29) but not the inversion.}

\subsection{Dose without a unit}
\label{sec:dose}
\note{C4. Dose has no transferable unit. The same fraction of the residual norm gives next-token
KL that differs about 25x between Qwen2.5-3B and -7B and about 480x between Qwen2.5-7B and
Gemma-3-4B at 0.25 of the norm, and about 4x across the three at 1.0. Calibrating the dose on
random directions does not transfer to real vectors, by an amount that depends on the recipe: at
a random-calibrated dose, real vectors move the next token 3--20x less than random directions on
Gemma-3-4B and about 1{,}000x less on Gemma-3-27B (S-1's concept, tail and sentence recipes),
while the released recipe on 27B moves it about as much as norm-matched random vectors (random
over real KL 0.3--2.3 at strengths 1--8, P2-R). One vector at one strength gives 0.0006 to 12 nats
depending on the prompt (S-1M at strength 4: factual-NO, introspective, story, neutral), so every
KL must name its prompt and recipe. Even a vector's own KL does not put models on one curve
(Table~\ref{tab:dose}, Figures~\ref{fig:dosefrac}, \ref{fig:dosekl}, \ref{fig:livekl}). The
released strength 4 sits past the dose at which Gemma-3-27B stops putting its first token on YES
or NO with the released prompt (median mass 0.93 at strength 2, $8\times10^{-5}$ at 4;
Figure~\ref{fig:p2r}a).}

\subsection{What detection measures at the published operating point}
\label{sec:operating}
\note{C5. What the first-token readout measures depends on strength, window and model. At alpha 6
(Study 3, older window, the filed primary) real and content-free vectors could not be separated
(random 0.305 against real 0.417, $p$ 0.33; equivalence inconclusive, S-4); at the published alpha
4 real beats random (0.504 against 0.252, $p$ 0.004). With the released prompt and window (P2-R,
filed), real vectors move the introspective answer more than impact-matched random ones at
strength 4 ($+16.3$ against $+10.9$ log-odds, $p = 1.9\times10^{-4}$); on the raw introspective
change the two are not separated at strengths 1 and 2 ($p$ 0.63, 0.22; content-free share 0.80
$[0.35, 1.23]$ at 2), and the difference appears once each vector's effect on the factual
questions is accounted for (C8). At the published strength the readout is read where the model
has mostly stopped answering YES or NO. The matched neutral prompt never answers YES or NO
(median mass $4\times10^{-8}$) and fails the filed mass check, so it supports no readout claim.
The same design on Qwen2.5-7B (P2-Q, filed) failed its manipulation check: the released prompt
never asks for YES or NO, and at strength 0 Qwen puts at most 1\% of its first-token probability
on either answer in any framing, so no endpoint was scored. With an answer instruction added
(P2-Q2, an amendment filed before its data) Qwen answers YES or NO (mass near 1), but its factual
controls fail: inside the injection frame it leans NO on half of thirty obvious-YES questions
(median log-odds $-0.39$, 15/30 leaning YES; factual-NO $-17.7$), so, as filed, no endpoint was
scored. In this frame Qwen answers the setup, not the question.}

\note{C8. At the doses where Gemma-3-27B still answers, real vectors add a framing-specific shift
toward YES that content-matched random vectors do not, and it almost never changes an answer. The
baseline-matched regression over the 90 factual items (P2, filed; bootstrap over concepts as
filed, Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}) gives an introspective excess of $+4.98$ $[+2.83, +8.81]$ at
strength 2 and $+6.90$ $[+4.88, +8.97]$ at 4, with a positive intercept for real vectors ($+1.30$
$[+0.11, +2.50]$ and $+3.99$ $[+2.12, +5.80]$) and none for impact-matched random vectors ($-0.35$,
$-0.26$). Paired per concept, real exceeds impact-matched random by $+4.64$ at strength 2
(Wilcoxon $p$ 0.003) and $+5.21$ at 4 ($p$ 0.005); against norm-matched random the difference is
smaller ($+1.03$, $+2.24$; $p$ 0.08 each). Restricted to items that still answer (YES+NO mass at
least 0.5 at strength 0 and at the strength scored), the introspective change at strength 2 is
$+3.99$ (16/30 items, $p$ 0.003). Yet the model answers YES on the introspective prompt for 1/30
concepts at strengths 1, 2 and 4. The filed P1 reads flattening at strength 4 (factual-YES
$-4.93$, $p$ 0.011) and an affirmative shift at strength 1 ($+1.07$, $p$ 0.014); among factual-YES
items still answering at strength 4 (16/30) the change is $-2.00$ ($p$ 0.40), so the flattening is
a change of margin on items that are collapsing, not of answers. Random vectors move factual-YES
further down than real ones at strengths 1--4 (impact-matched $-3.6$, $-8.5$, $-14.5$), not at 8.
Exploratory except where marked filed; computed after the second internal review
(Table~\ref{tab:p2r}, Figure~\ref{fig:p2r}).}

\note{P2-F's own log-odds reading, kept for the prompt it used: injection moves confident
factual-NO answers more than the introspective answer (Gemma-3-27B, S-1M prompt, strength 4:
$+14.75$ against $+5.45$, 27/30 concepts), while the neutral prompt barely moves
(Table~\ref{tab:logodds}, Figure~\ref{fig:logodds}). The one framing-specific signal on Qwen is
at the gentlest dose (tail vectors at 0.25 of the norm).}

\section{A reporting checklist}
\label{sec:checklist}
\note{C7 as practice: a steering gate per vector before any detection number; real-vector KL and
coherence at every dose, with the full dose curve; content-free, impact-matched and span
controls; a neutral framing and factual-YES, factual-NO and contested controls with a
baseline-matched comparison; YES+NO mass at every dose beside any log-odds; liveness rules
validated against random vectors; first-token readouts beside generated text;
never a dose in raw units or fraction of norm alone. Logit-lens accessibility as a cheap screen.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\note{Free-tier hardware: 4-bit for 12B and 27B; the precision axis is untestable on T4s.
Open models up to 27B, no closed models. 30 concepts. The steering gate is one rule; a stricter
rule belongs in an appendix. No judge-scored numbers. P2-R was scored once, but three of its
filed secondaries (random-vector P1 and P2, the identify-gate agreement) were added to the scorer
after its primary output was seen. Negative results kept: S-1's criterion,
P2-G, P2-M's secondary.}

\section{Conclusion}
\note{The instrument, not introspection, is what this paper measures; every assumption fails in
some regime and the standard checks do not notice.}
