\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\note{The paradigm: inject a concept vector, ask the model whether it detects an injected
thought \citep{lindsey2025introspection,macar2026mechanisms}. Three assumptions every such result
rests on, none checked in the literature. What the paper does: an audit of the instrument, not
a verdict on introspection. Contributions C1--C8 from the outline, one line each.}

\note{Positioning in one sentence: five critiques exist
\citep{hahami2025disturbance,lederman2026contentagnostic,singh2026realitycheck,wu2026steerable};
this paper's contribution is the instrument (liveness, dose, health checks), not another
verdict.}

\section{The audit protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\note{Models: Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Gemma-3-4B/12B/27B, Gemma-2-2B for A-F1. Recipes: concept token,
template tail, sentence mean, released. Controls: norm-matched random, impact-matched random,
shuffled, span. Doses: fraction of residual norm, KL-calibrated grid, released raw strengths.
Readouts: steering gate (rule-scored), first-token \pyes{}, rule-scored text. Live rule: steered
at the gate dose and not at dose 0. Every arm pre-registered; amendments and deviations in
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}. Hardware: free T4s, 4-bit for 12B and 27B.}

\subsection{Liveness, and the checks that should catch it}
\label{sec:liveness}
\note{C2. Whether a vector steers at all depends on model and read position: template-tail
vectors are inert on Qwen, the KL-calibrated 27B grid stopped below the dose onset, and the
released recipe at its released strength is live on Gemma-3-27B (Table~\ref{tab:liveness}).
Present as the failed S-1 prediction, with its 12B reversal under a literal-word steering rule.}

\note{C1. Pooled, some checks look useful: distinctness (0.78, 0.82, 0.73) and logit-lens
accessibility (0.69, 0.77, 0.62) (Table~\ref{tab:health}, Figure~\ref{fig:health}). Holding recipe
and concept type fixed, none is consistent (Table~\ref{tab:cells}); the pooled signal comes
from recipes and concept types that differ in both the check and liveness. Raw norm sits at
chance once concept type is fixed; the steering rule scores abstract concepts live almost never.
At the published point no check finds the six dead released vectors (low power). A second
liveness rule, the identify stage's rise of at least 2 nats in $\log P(\text{concept})$, is not
specific: real vectors pass it 27/30 at strength 4 and random vectors 21/30 (26/30 at 8), and it
agrees with the steering gate at Cohen's $\kappa$ 0.10 (P2-R, filed secondaries).}

\subsection{The inverted \pyes{} check}
\label{sec:inverted}
\note{C3. On Qwen a significant \pyes{} shift predicts a dead vector, not a live one (AUC
0.367, 0.353; concrete concepts only 0.337, 0.329; Fisher-combined $p$ 0.0019, not significant
per session after Holm). It sits in the concept arm on both models and in the tail arm only on
Qwen-7B. A direct output-steering path explains part of the shift (P2-M, $\rho$ 0.26, 0.29) but
not the inversion.}

\subsection{Dose without a unit}
\label{sec:dose}
\note{C4. At the same norm, real vectors move the next token 3--20x less than random directions
on Gemma-3-4B and about 1{,}000x less on 27B. The same fraction of the residual norm gives KL
that differs about 480x across families at 0.25 and about 4x at 1.0. One vector at one strength
gives 0.0006 to 12 nats depending on the prompt (S-1M at strength 4: factual-NO, introspective,
story, neutral), so every KL must name its prompt. Even a vector's own KL does not put models on
one curve (Table~\ref{tab:dose}, Figures~\ref{fig:dosefrac}, \ref{fig:dosekl}, \ref{fig:livekl}).
The released strength 4 sits past the dose at which Gemma-3-27B stops answering YES or NO with
the released prompt (median YES+NO mass 0.93 at strength 2, $8\times10^{-5}$ at 4;
Figure~\ref{fig:p2r}a).}

\subsection{What detection measures at the published operating point}
\label{sec:operating}
\note{C5. What the first-token readout measures depends on strength and window. At alpha 6
(Study 3, older window, the filed primary) content-free vectors produce the whole shift (random
0.305 against real 0.417, $p$ 0.33); at the published alpha 4 real beats random (0.504 against
0.252, $p$ 0.004). With the released prompt and window (P2-R, filed), real beats impact-matched
random only where the model has mostly stopped answering: at strength 4, $+16.3$ against $+10.9$
log-odds ($p = 1.9\times10^{-4}$, content-free share 0.67). At strengths 1 and 2, where YES and NO
keep 0.997 and 0.93 of the mass, the two cannot be told apart ($p$ 0.63, 0.22; shares 0.76,
0.80). The neutral prompt barely responds (injected against uninjected AUC 0.56--0.63 at
strengths 1--4; Table~\ref{tab:p2r}).}

\note{C8. Injection flattens confident answers; it does not add a global yes-bias (P2-R P1,
filed). At strength 4 factual-YES moves down by 4.93 log-odds ($p$ 0.011), after a small rise at
strength 1 ($+1.07$, $p$ 0.014); impact-matched random vectors flatten it more at every strength
($-3.6$ already at strength 1). The introspective answer moves more than a regression on the 90
factual items predicts at its baseline (P2, filed): $+4.98$ $[+2.57, +9.13]$ at strength 2 and
$+6.90$ $[+3.22, +10.24]$ at strength 4. Most of that excess is not concept-specific:
norm-matched random vectors give $+3.44$ $[+1.73, +5.67]$ and $+4.79$ $[+2.65, +6.76]$, and
impact-matched ones $+0.90$ and $+2.76$ with intervals spanning zero (filed secondaries, scored
after the primary output was seen). With the released prompt the raw ordering differs from
P2-F's (introspective $+16.3$, factual-NO $+11.2$ at strength 4); once the baseline is regressed
out the introspective answer moves more in both. At strength 4 every log-odds compares two tokens
the model is no longer producing, so strength 2 is the operating point that supports a readout
claim. On the probability scale factual-YES does not move (median 0, $p$ 0.063), the same
artefact P2-F's withdrawn reading rested on (Table~\ref{tab:p2r}, Figure~\ref{fig:p2r}).}

\note{P2-F's own log-odds reading, kept for the prompt it used: injection moves confident
factual-NO answers more than the introspective answer (Gemma-3-27B, S-1M prompt, strength 4:
$+14.75$ against $+5.45$, 27/30 concepts), while the neutral prompt barely moves
(Table~\ref{tab:logodds}, Figure~\ref{fig:logodds}). The one framing-specific signal on Qwen is
at the gentlest dose (tail vectors at 0.25 of the norm).}

\section{A reporting checklist}
\label{sec:checklist}
\note{C7 as practice: a steering gate per vector before any detection number; real-vector KL and
coherence at every dose, with the full dose curve; content-free, impact-matched and span
controls; a neutral framing and factual-YES, factual-NO and contested controls with a
baseline-matched comparison; YES+NO mass at every dose beside any log-odds; liveness rules
validated against random vectors; first-token readouts beside generated text;
never a dose in raw units or fraction of norm alone. Logit-lens accessibility as a cheap screen.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\note{Free-tier hardware: 4-bit for 12B and 27B; the precision axis is untestable on T4s.
Open models up to 27B, no closed models. 30 concepts. The steering gate is one rule; a stricter
rule belongs in an appendix. No judge-scored numbers. P2-R was scored once, but three of its
filed secondaries (random-vector P1 and P2, the identify-gate agreement) were added to the scorer
after its primary output was seen. Negative results kept: S-1's criterion,
P2-G, P2-M's secondary.}
